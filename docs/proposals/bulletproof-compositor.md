# Bulletproof compositor: acceptance criteria, what the best compositors do, and what I ran (research and tests, part 4)

**Written:** 2026-10-05 (the fuzzing and builds on that day; the soak ran in chunks from the evening of 2026-10-05 into 2026-10-06). **Status:** research and proposal. Nothing here is a decision, and nothing here changes `HUB-OS.md`, the image, or any program in this repository. Everything that belongs to the owner is collected in section 12. This continues `docs/proposals/hub-stability.md` (read first) and uses its patch files and scripts in `docs/proposals/hub-stability/`.

## 0. Labels and what this is

Every item carries one label, as in `hub-stability.md`: **TESTED** (I ran a command in this session; the command and a trimmed result are given or are in the files named), **SOURCE** (read in a file or on a web page, with the path or link; today is 2026-10-05), **BELIEVED** (my reasoning, not shown), **UNKNOWN** (nobody here knows). "Pristine" is driftwm at the pinned commit `352333a8fa1b22171492d4b71a54102045c9a19d` built unchanged; "patched" is that plus the patches in `docs/proposals/bulletproof-compositor/patches/` (and `hub-stability`'s P1 to P5 and P6, which they sit on).

**I used no sub-helpers; this document was written by a helper agent of the lead.** Every line of source and every web page named here I read myself with `curl`, `grep` or the file reader; the survey in section 6 is from raw source files, not from summaries. Where I could not reach a source, the text says UNKNOWN.

**Where it ran.** The same cloud machine as before (4 shared CPUs, other helpers running, load average 5 to 14, no GPU, no `/dev/kvm`, no DRM device, no systemd): driftwm **nested** in a virtual X server (`Xvfb`), software OpenGL (Mesa llvmpipe 25.2.8), `foot` terminals as stand-in viewers, no real `hubd`. Disk was tight (3 to 9 GB free, shared); build folders were deleted as soon as they were no longer needed.

## 1. The short version (plain words)

**Is the compositor bulletproof? No, not at the pinned commit, and I can only show "much better" for the patched build, not "bulletproof".** Nothing can be proved bulletproof by testing; what testing can do is find the ways it breaks and show that they stop. This document does that and lists, for eight measurable goals, the number to reach, what was shown so far, and what is still open.

**Ten findings (each is explained and labelled later):**

1. **A new fuzzer finds three ways to kill or freeze the unpatched compositor in minutes.** TESTED: of 10 fuzz runs of 100 seconds against the unmodified build, **10 died** (5 by BC-2, 5 by BC-3 below), and a third bug (BC-1) freezes it for ever. `hub-stability.md` reported "no crash found in 12 minutes" because its fuzzer skipped the requests that trigger them and only checked that the process still existed. All three bugs are in **Smithay** (the library under driftwm) or in Mesa, not in driftwm's own code, and the Smithay one is still in its master branch today (SOURCE, read 2026-10-05).
2. **BC-1, a freeze from a five-line client.** A popup that names itself as its own parent makes the compositor's single thread spin at 100 % for ever; it stops answering Wayland and IPC, and **ignores SIGTERM**. Only `kill -9` ends it. TESTED. With the hub-stability hang policy it was found after 35 s and fully recovered 3.1 s after the kill (TESTED, section 7.4).
3. **BC-2, a crash from a twelve-line client.** A subsurface told to place itself above itself makes the compositor panic (exit 101). TESTED.
4. **BC-3, a crash that is not even a panic.** A viewport destination or a subsurface offset near the 32-bit limit makes Mesa's software renderer abort the process with "stack smashing detected". TESTED in software rendering; whether the real GPU driver does the same is UNKNOWN.
5. **With the patches P7 to P11 and D1 to D5 (about 200 changed lines in all), the same fuzzer ran 27,089 connections and 2,231,793 requests (16 runs of 120 s, build p25) without a crash, hang or sanitizer report** in the release build, and 10,648 connections and 1,037,069 requests (8 runs of 100 s) in an AddressSanitizer build that also checks integer overflow. The unmodified build dies within 100 seconds. TESTED. That is evidence, not a proof (section 5.1 says what the fuzzer cannot reach). One caveat found in the final round: the IPC fuzzer, run with a 6 GB memory ceiling, made the compositor abort on a failed allocation in all 3 seeds (BC-14); without the ceiling it ran 103,056 requests and stayed alive. I could not reduce it to a small reproduction.
6. **Saved state held.** **3,200 `kill -9` of the compositor (two runs, 1,701 on build p20 and 1499 on p24), and after every one `session.json` parsed and the restored windows were a state the compositor really had** (1,161 at the one-second save boundary, 322 while the process was held inside the write). TESTED. A kill in the middle of a write left a half-written `.tmp` file and an intact `session.json` every time (316 of the 322 held-write kills had a `.tmp` file on disk at the moment of the kill). A power cut is a different case and is UNKNOWN (section 7.5).
7. **Two ways to lose or poison saved state without a crash were found.** (a) A `session.json` with 30,000 entries makes the compositor use 9.3 GB and get killed at every start (BC-5, patched with a cap of 512 entries). (b) While a config without `restore_windows` is active, the next window change rewrites `session.json` without the live windows, so a crash then restores nothing (BC-8, not patched: a design question). TESTED.
8. **Config is a weak point.** One unknown field makes the whole file ignored at start-up (BC-7), a NUL character in a key binding or keyboard layout crashes the compositor (BC-6, patched), and hot reload is the path that turns a config typo into a crash of the running desktop. TESTED.
9. **Sanitizers and Miri:** AddressSanitizer found no memory error in driftwm or Smithay in 10,648 connections and 1,037,069 requests (8 runs of 100 s) of fuzzing. ThreadSanitizer: 10 warnings in 4 runs, all at mutex or condition destruction inside Mesa (BELIEVED a known false-positive pattern, not proved). Miri ran **134 tests of the canvas, layout and session modules with no undefined behaviour**; the `unsafe` blocks of driftwm itself call C libraries and cannot run under Miri, so they were read, not executed. MemorySanitizer: 135 library tests of the session, canvas and layout modules passed with 0 reports; whole-program MemorySanitizer is not possible because the C libraries are not instrumented.
10. **The soak (the owner asked for at least 8 hours, in 2-hour chunks):** 8.2 hours in 3 chunks (chunk 0 on p22 stopped by hand at 4.7 h, the others 2 h each on the final p25): 0 unexpected compositor exits; 26 of 26 quiescent `kill -9` restored every window to the exact position and size (the window order differed in 2 of them, cause UNKNOWN, question 14); live windows equal after relaunch 1120 ok, 0 bad. The 11 random-kill "mismatches" come from the checker (section 7.1; BELIEVED). TESTED. The 8-hour minimum is not yet met (a chunk is still running); the 72-hour gate is not.

**What is still not shown:** anything on real hardware (GPU reset, hotplug, a real disk or power cut), anything about the real viewers, and anything about code the fuzzer does not reach. Section 8 lists them.

## 4. Findings: crashes, hangs and hazards, with minimal reproductions and patches

Numbering "BC-n" is new in this document (hub-stability used F1 to F10). Every reproduction is a small script in `docs/proposals/bulletproof-compositor/scripts/`; the runner `reprorun.sh BIN LABEL CMD...` starts the compositor, runs the script, then says whether the compositor is alive, hung or dead (and takes a backtrace with `gdb` if hung). "Pristine" means the unmodified driftwm at the pin, built in release mode (`driftwm-pristine`). "Patched" means the build named in section 3.

**Why hub-stability did not see BC-1 to BC-3.** Its fuzzer (`fuzz.py`) skipped `damage` and the size requests (they trip a debug assertion), checked only that the process id was alive and a health check afterwards, and sent mostly invalid object ids so most connections were killed within 150 requests. **What each patch does (all small; every one is in `docs/proposals/bulletproof-compositor/patches/`):**

- P7 (Smithay): `GetPopup` refuses a parent chain that contains the popup's own surface (protocol error `invalid_popup_parent`, only that client is disconnected); `find_popup_root_surface` and `get_popup_toplevel_coords` stop after 256 steps; the latter no longer `unwrap`s a popup that has no parent. 58 changed lines.
- P8 (Smithay): `reorder` returns an error (so the client gets `bad_surface`) when the sibling is the surface itself. 5 lines.
- P9 (Smithay): `wp_viewport.set_destination` above 2^24 is a protocol error `bad_value`; `wl_subsurface.set_position` is clamped to plus or minus 2^24. **Policy**: the protocol allows larger numbers; 2^24 is my choice and is question 4.
- P10 (Smithay): every number in an `xdg_positioner` request is clamped to plus or minus 2^24 (parent size to 0 to 2^24). Same policy question.
- P11 (Smithay): negative sizes in `wl_region.add/subtract` and `wl_surface.damage` are ignored; negative `xdg_toplevel.set_min_size/set_max_size` is the protocol error `invalid_size`; `zwlr_layer_surface_v1.set_size` is limited to 2^24.

- D2 (driftwm): a NUL in a key combination is an error; a NUL in a keyboard setting is a warning and the default is used.
- D3 (driftwm): saturating arithmetic for the corner rectangles built from a client's opaque region.
- D5 (driftwm): saturating arithmetic (and floating-point sums instead of 32-bit sums) in the canvas helpers and the region decomposition that BC-11 and BC-13 name. It covers the helpers the fuzzer showed; it is not a review of every `+` in the program (clippy counts 717 sites that may overflow, `hub-stability` 5.1).

## 5. What I ran: fuzzing, sanitizers, Miri, the `unsafe` blocks

### 5.1 The new client fuzzer (`fuzz2.py`) and what it covers

**What it is (TESTED to run; code in `scripts/fuzz2.py`).** A Python program that speaks the Wayland wire protocol over the compositor's socket. It reads 100 protocol XML files (the Wayland core, `wayland-protocols` stable/staging/unstable, the wlroots and plasma protocols, the misc protocols; the same families `fuzz.py` used) and does the following that the old one did not:

- it answers `xdg_wm_base.ping` and acknowledges every `xdg_surface` and `zwlr_layer_surface_v1` configure, so windows really map;
- hand-written scenarios build valid-looking state: shm pool + buffer + attach + damage + commit; toplevels with every state request; popups with a full positioner; subsurfaces with sync/desync and re-stacking; viewports; fractional scale; layer surfaces; data-device selection and drag; primary selection; activation tokens; pointer constraints and relative pointers; cursor surfaces and cursor shapes; decorations; destroy of a random object;
- 40 % of the steps are generic random requests whose arguments are valid enum values and existing objects most of the time (the rest: edge integers, odd strings, NUL, short payloads, trailing junk, out-of-range opcodes);
- connection modes: normal (70 %), flip (a bit flipped in a valid message, 10 %), noread (sends and never reads, 6 %), torn (partial headers and absurd sizes, 5 %), fdflood (hundreds of file descriptors in one message, 5 %), garbage (random bytes, 4 %);
- after **every** connection it probes the event loop with a fresh `wl_display.sync` (4 s, then 6 s) so a hang is found at once; for a crash or a hang it saves the raw messages of the last connections (`crash-*.json`, `hang-*.json`) for `replay.py` and `ddmin.py` (delta debugging; the minimal message list for BC-3 was produced this way).

**Requests covered and not covered.** `covreport.py` merges the `coverage-*.json` files of the runs listed in section 3 and counts, for every interface the compositor advertises (and every interface that an advertised one can create), which requests were sent. The table is in `results/coverage.md`: **302 requests on 110 reachable interfaces (from 47 advertised globals); 301 (100 %) sent at least once, 277 (92 %) at least 20 times, 1 never sent** (`ext_image_copy_capture_frame_v1.damage_buffer`; it needs a frame in flight and the fuzzer does not get that far). Merged from 40 runs of the release and ASan builds. `hub-stability` did not report a count.

What the table does **not** say: "sent" is not "reached the deep code". A request counts if it was written to the socket; the fuzzer does not know if the compositor accepted it, only if the connection died afterwards. Requests that need a real input device event first (pointer/keyboard grabs with a real serial, `start_drag` with a real button press, `xdg_toplevel.move/resize` with a real serial, pointer constraints that need pointer focus, `wl_data_device` offers that need keyboard focus) were sent with random serials and so mostly produced protocol errors or no-ops. The input paths driven by libinput and DRM events are **not** covered by this fuzzer at all (there is no input device here); they are covered only by `xdotool` input to the nested window in the soak.

**Not covered by any fuzzer I ran:** `ext_session_lock` after the lock is granted (the nested window has no lock surface per output that the fuzzer could satisfy; the compositor held a lock in some runs and kept running), screencopy / image-capture flows that need a real frame, `xdg_foreign`/`xdg_exporter` handshakes with a second client, the Xwayland satellite (not installed), input-method and text-input flows, virtual-keyboard keymaps with valid XKB text, dmabuf import (no GPU), and anything that needs two cooperating clients.

## 7. Soak, kills, clock jumps, hang drill, saved state

### 7.1 The long soak (`soak2.py`)

**What it is.** Nested driftwm (the patched build (chunk 0: `driftwm-p22`; chunks 1 and later: the final candidate `driftwm-p25`), release, software rendering, 1920x1080 virtual screen, no `HS_CATCH_PANICS`, so a panic would be a real exit), up to 20 `foot` windows, run by a Python driver under `nice -n 19` that is also the supervisor (it restarts the compositor 1.0 s after an exit, as the s6 model of `hub-stability` does). One operation every 0.3 to 1.5 s, chosen at random: open (26 %), close, client crash (`kill -9` of the viewer), client freeze (`SIGSTOP`, resumed after 25 s), move, resize, focus, camera, zoom, bookmark set/delete, suspend, relaunch, **layout save and layout apply** (a hubd-style snapshot of every window's place and a later replay of it through the IPC socket), mouse and key input through `xdotool`, and the faults below. Every 60 s it records the compositor's memory, open files, threads, CPU, IPC latency, window counts, the size and entry count of `session.json`, and the load average. Every 10 s the hang probe asks twice (IPC `State` and a Wayland `wl_display.sync`); both must fail for 30 s before it kills. Faults:

- **misbehaving clients**: a `fuzz2.py` burst of 6, 12 or 20 s about every 30 operations, in a random mode (default mix, no-read flood, garbage plus torn messages, normal, fd flood);
- **config reload attempts** every 1 to 4 minutes: a valid change, a bad colour, a multi-byte colour (the F3 bug of `hub-stability`), garbage bytes, an empty file, a deleted file, bad TOML, a 1.2 MB file, unknown keys, a file written in two parts; then the good file is put back (and a window nudged, see BC-8);
- **`kill -9` of the compositor**, two kinds. *Quiescent*: no operation for 7 s (longer than the 5 s camera save delay), the exact state is recorded, the compositor is killed, restarted after 1 s, and the restored stand-ins must equal the recorded state **exactly** (position, size, order); then every stand-in is relaunched and 3 s later the live windows must equal them. *Random moment*: killed straight after an operation; every restored stand-in must equal a state seen in the previous 12 s;
- **saved-state corruption before a restart** about hourly: `session.json` truncated, empty, random bytes, NULs, 30,000 entries, a wrong version number, replaced by a directory, a half-written `.tmp` left behind; the compositor must start and the bad file must be set aside.

**Result.** **8.2 hours in 3 chunks (still going when this was assembled); the owner asked for at least 8 hours, in 2-hour chunks, with the results committed after each chunk.** TESTED. Chunk 0 started as one long run (planned 12 h, build p22) and was stopped by hand after 4.7 h when the chunk rule arrived; its logs are kept. Chunks 1 and later run the final candidate **p25**.

### 7.2 Thousands of `kill -9` (`killtest.py`)

8 stand-ins, a persistent `session.json`. Each cycle: start the compositor, change 1 to 4 things (move, resize, focus, camera), then `kill -9` it at one of three moments: **rand** (0 to 2.5 s after the last change), **edge** (1.0 s plus or minus 0.12 s after the last change: the one-second save delay, where the write happens), or **strace** (the compositor runs under `strace -f -P session.json.tmp -e inject=write:delay_enter=600ms`, which holds every write to the temporary file for 0.6 s; the test waits until the `.tmp` file exists and kills the compositor **while it is stuck inside the write**). After each kill: `session.json` must parse (version 2); the compositor is restarted after 1 s and its stand-ins must equal a state it really had (one of the states after each change, or the state before them). A "stale after the debounce" counter checks that a kill more than 1.4 s after the last change restores the newest state.

**Result.** **Run 1 (p20): 1,701 kills** (912 rand, 617 edge, 172 strace-held write, 169 with a `.tmp` file seen): `session.json` valid and the restore equal to a real state in every one. It stopped by itself when the virtual X server died (see section 8, mistakes). **Run 2 (p24): 1,499 kills** (805 rand, 544 edge, 150 strace-held write, 147 with a `.tmp` file seen), 1173 restores equal to the newest state and 326 equal to the one before it (allowed: the kill came inside the save delay), start failures: 1 (one start failed with `XOpenDisplayFailed`, which is the test rig, not the compositor). **Total 3,200 kills, 0 unreadable files, 0 restores that were not a real state.** The A7 criterion asks for 5,000 kills; 3,200 is 64 % of that, so A7 is **not** fully met.

### 7.3 Restoring twice (`relaunchtwice.py`)

12 stand-ins; relaunch them all (live windows adopt the places), wait 8 s, `kill -9`, restart, compare, repeat 3 times with the same file. **TESTED (pristine build): 12 of 12 windows equal to the reference after every restart and after every relaunch, `session.json` equal to the live windows each time, in all 3 rounds.** (A first version of the hang drill, which resized live windows through IPC before suspending them, showed 4 of 20 windows with the default size after the restore; that is the IPC `Resize` answering with the requested size before the client has committed it, not a restore error, and I do not count it.)

### 7.4 A real hang, detected and recovered (`hangdrill.py`, criterion A6)

The 5-line popup client of BC-1 against the pristine build, with 20 live windows, the hub-stability probe policy (probe every 2 s, kill after 4 failed rounds and 30 s, restart after 1 s). Raw output in `results/hangdrill-pristine.txt`.

| Event | Time |
|---|---|
| hang triggered | 0 s |
| both probes failing (first failed round) | after 7 s |
| compositor killed (`State: R (running)`, a busy loop) | **35.1 s** after the first failed round (42.2 s after the trigger: rounds are about 7 s each because the probes' own time-outs add to the 2 s wait) |
| IPC answers again | **1.35 s** after the kill (includes the 1.0 s restart delay) |
| 20 of 20 stand-ins restored, relaunched, all windows live | **3.1 s** after the kill |

So the policy detects a real hang in 35 s and restores in 3 s. Against the criterion A6 (35 s, 5 s) it just meets the numbers. Two cautions: (1) 35 s is the *minimum* the policy allows; the longer rounds with real probes are what you get. (2) The time with no working desktop is 42 s plus 3 s for this bug. With BC-1 patched this hang does not exist, but any future hang costs the same. `SIGTERM` does nothing against such a hang (TESTED), so the supervisor must use `SIGKILL`.

### 7.5 Saved state

- **Writes are atomic against a process kill (TESTED).** See 7.2: 316 of the 322 held-write kills had a `.tmp` file on disk at the moment of the kill
- **Corrupt, oversized, wrong or missing files do not stop a start (TESTED in the soak, 8 kinds).** 9 corruption tests had run (all chunks) when this was assembled (the soak does one about hourly: truncated, empty, random bytes, NULs, 30,000 entries, wrong version, a directory in place of the file, a half-written `.tmp`); after every one the compositor started and the bad file was set aside. One kind was a real failure on the pristine build: 30,000 entries (BC-5, patched by D1).
- **Power loss (UNKNOWN).** `session.json` is written without `fsync` (SOURCE `session_store.rs:453`: "Never fsync'd: it would block the main loop once a second through a drag or a pan"). The comment is right that a process kill cannot lose the data; a power cut can. The cost of `fsync` in the compositor's thread is the reason it is off; writing from a worker thread was suggested in `hub-stability` (F5). Not changed, not tested (needs hardware): section 8.
- **A config change can erase the saved windows without a crash (BC-8, TESTED).** See section 4.
- **The restore chain keeps exactness over repeated crashes (TESTED, 7.3).**

### 7.6 Clock jumps (`clocktest.py`, `libfaketime`)

**What I did.** I cannot change the system clock. I unpacked `libfaketime` 0.9.10 with `dpkg -x` and ran driftwm under `LD_PRELOAD` with `FAKETIME_TIMESTAMP_FILE` pointing at a file that I rewrite while the program runs (with `FAKETIME_NO_CACHE=1`), which changes what `clock_gettime` returns to the program. Phase R fakes only the wall clock (`CLOCK_REALTIME`, what an NTP step, a dead RTC battery or a manual date change change; `FAKETIME_DONT_FAKE_MONOTONIC=1`). Phase M fakes the monotonic clock as well, which Linux never does (a stress test, out of spec). After each jump the test moves a stand-in and waits for the change to appear in `session.json` (the 1-second save delay), starts a camera animation and waits for it to finish, and reads the compositor's IPC. Jumps: +1 h, -1 h, +10 years, -30 years, to 1970-01-01 00:00:10, to 2038-01-19 03:14:00 (just before 2^31), to 2106-02-07 06:28:00 (just before 2^32), to year 2300, +1 s, -1 s, back to real time, then 40 flips of 1 to 3 seconds, then `kill -9` with the clock in year 2300 and a restart on the real clock. Raw output: `results/clock-p23.txt`, `results/clock-pristine.txt`.

**Result (TESTED, both the pristine and the patched build).**

| | Phase R: wall clock only | Phase M: monotonic clock faked too |
|---|---|---|
| IPC answers | yes, 0.4 to 15 ms after every jump | yes |
| Save delay after a window change | **1.01 to 1.02 s after every jump** (the same as with no jump) | 1.01 to 1.06 s |
| Camera animation finishes | yes, every time (0.0 to 0.9 s) | **No in 6 of 11 jumps** ("not within 8 s") after a monotonic jump backwards or after a prior jump into the far future; fine after a jump forwards |
| `saved_at` in the file | follows the faked wall clock, including 9 (1970), 2147483639, 4294967279 and 10413791999 | same |
| Panic or crash | none | none |
| 40 flips of 1 to 3 s | alive | alive |
| `kill -9` with the clock in year 2300, restart on the real clock | started, 5 of 5 stand-ins restored | same |

**Reading (labelled).** SOURCE: driftwm uses the monotonic clock (`std::time::Instant`) for every timer; `SystemTime` appears only in the informational `saved_at` field and in the name of a quarantined file (`session.rs`, `desktop_entry.rs`). TESTED (phase R): so a wall-clock step of any size, including past 2038 and 2106, changes nothing but the number in `saved_at`. BELIEVED: the animation stalls in phase M because the animation deadlines are `Instant`s that the faked clock moved backwards; on Linux the monotonic clock never goes backwards, so this does not apply to the real hub. One small thing to know (SOURCE `session.rs:quarantine`): the quarantined file's name contains the wall-clock seconds, so two quarantines in the same second (a clock stuck or stepped back) overwrite each other; the evidence of the older bad file is lost. What the test did **not** cover: the kernel's own timers (`timerfd`, `epoll` time-outs, the file system's modification times) are not shifted by `libfaketime`; `hubd` (Go reads the clock through the vDSO, which `LD_PRELOAD` does not reach); and the time-zone database.

## 8. What only real hardware can prove

Nothing in this list could be run here; each is a test for December (the first five also appear in `hub-stability.md` section 11).

1. **GPU reset and device loss (criterion A5).** Trigger a reset on purpose (10 times, with the full desktop up) and record: does driftwm die, stay alive and blind, or keep drawing; how long is the screen black; does a new process get a working GPU context; what do the viewers do. The code reading in `hub-stability.md` 8.2 says driftwm retries a dead renderer for ever and requests no robust context, so "alive and blind" is the expected outcome. This is the one place where sway and KWin do better than driftwm (section 6). Everything I measured about restart time (3 s) assumes a new process gets a working GPU; that is UNKNOWN until this test.
2. **Whether BC-3 happens on the real GPU.** The "stack smashing" abort was found in Mesa's software renderer. Send `wp_viewport.set_destination(2147483647, 2147483647)` and a subsurface offset of 2^30 to the unpatched build on the real GPU driver. If it does not crash there, P9 is only protection for the software fallback; if it does, P9 is needed.
3. **Projector off and on, cable pulled (hotplug)** with zero outputs: what driftwm does with no output and when one returns (O3 in `hub-stability.md`). The session-save code removes a baseline for a vanished output and re-seeds on replug (SOURCE `session_store.rs`), but nothing ran.
4. **Power cut during a write.** `session.json` is never `fsync`ed (SOURCE, `session_store.rs:453`), so after a power cut the newest write can be lost, and on some file systems a renamed file can come back empty. The kill tests (section 7) prove the file is never half-written when only the process dies, because the kernel keeps the page cache; they say nothing about power loss. Test: pull the plug 100 times during a window-moving loop, on the real root file system and mount options, and check that the file is the old or the new version, never empty.
5. **Real viewers.** Their reconnect time after a compositor restart, their memory use, their use of protocols the `foot` stand-ins do not use (viewporter, dmabuf, subsurfaces, text input, shortcut-inhibit); a viewer that leaks or hangs. Every fuzz and soak run here used `foot` and a script.
6. **Real input devices.** libinput events, key repeat, touch, tablets, a hot-unplugged keyboard, the 1,000 Hz mouse of the gaming box through the forwarder. The input path (`src/input/*`) was exercised here only by `xdotool` into the nested window and was not fuzzed. The 102 `get_pointer().unwrap()`-style sites rely on the pointer, keyboard and touch being added once and never removed (`hub-stability.md` 5.2, class A); with real hardware hotplug that is still true because the seat devices are fixed at start-up, but that has not been run.
7. **Real display backend.** `--backend udev` was never run (no DRM device); the nested `winit` path differs in the code that creates outputs, frames and page flips. Every number in this document is for the nested path.
8. **Heat, fans, long runs.** A soak of days to weeks, memory fragmentation with real window content, the OOM killer with a real memory limit, thermal throttling. The soak here totals about 8 hours or more (section 7.1), a ninth of the 72-hour gate.
9. **Clock steps from real NTP** and a real RTC (the test used `libfaketime`, which changes what the program sees, not what the kernel does; `timerfd` and `epoll` deadlines in the kernel are not shifted by it).
10. **`hubd`'s own clock handling.** Go's runtime reads the clock through the kernel's vDSO, which `LD_PRELOAD` cannot intercept, so the Go side was not tested at all (UNKNOWN). A time-namespace or a VM is needed.

3. **The numbers I chose, as policy.** P9, P10 and P11 limit viewport destinations, subsurface offsets, positioner numbers and layer sizes to 2^24 (16,777,216) pixels; D1 refuses a `session.json` with more than 512 entries; D4 limits a stand-in's chrome buffers to 16,384 pixels wide. 4. **Acceptance thresholds in section 2.** Do you accept A1 to A9 as the definition of "bulletproof enough" for the hub, or change any number: a 72-hour soak (A4), 24 hours of fuzzing per release candidate (A2), 5,000 kills including 100 inside a write (A7, A8), a 10-second GPU recovery (A5), a 35-second hang detection (A6)? Who runs them at each driftwm bump (a machine in the cluster; they take about two days)?
5. **Config safety (BC-6, BC-7, BC-8).** (a) A config with one unknown field makes the *starting* compositor use built-in defaults (all your settings gone, including `restore_windows`) but a *running* compositor keeps its old settings. Which do you want at start-up: defaults (today), refuse to start, or fall back to the last config that worked? (b) A valid config without `restore_windows = true` erases the saved windows at the next window change. Should the hub's build force `restore_windows` on, or should `hubd` own all window restore so driftwm's switch does not matter (the proposal in `driftwm-layouts.md`)? (c) Turn config hot reload off on the hub (`hub-stability` question 4)? The new evidence: two of the three config problems act only through hot reload or start-up, never through a crash.
6. **Per-client memory (BC-9).** A client's buffers cost the compositor about 8 times their size, and nothing limits a client. Do you want a per-window or per-client limit in the compositor (for example refuse a buffer above 64 MiB, a 4096x4096 window), or rely on the cgroup limit for the viewer group that `hub-stability` 7.6 proposes?
7. **Features the hub does not need but the defaults advertise** (`hub-stability` question 8, now with a concrete reason): `ext_session_lock` leaves the outputs blank if the locking client dies (BC-10), and screen capture, output power, gamma, virtual keyboard and touch are code that can fail. May we hide them from viewers (or remove them), starting with the session lock?
8. **Hang and GPU policy.** With BC-1 a hang took 35 s to detect plus 3 s to restore, and `SIGTERM` does nothing against it, so the supervisor must use `SIGKILL`. Do you accept that, or want the probe window shortened (it must stay above the longest GPU reset, which is unknown until December)? For a GPU reset, should driftwm rebuild its renderer in place like sway and KWin (a large change), or exit after 2 s of failed frames and be restarted (`hub-stability` P10, about 15 lines)?
9. **`fsync` of `session.json`.** Today it is never synced, so a power cut can lose the newest write or, on some file systems, leave an empty file. Add an `fsync` from a worker thread (a code change, small cost)? Or accept the risk because the hub keeps its layouts in `hubd` anyway (`driftwm-layouts.md`)?
10. **Catching panics (`hub-stability` P3).** Of the six ways found here to take down or freeze the release build, three were panics that a catch-and-continue would have survived (BC-2, BC-6, BC-12), but BC-1 (a hang), BC-3 (an abort inside Mesa) and BC-5 (running out of memory) are not panics and no catch helps. Is "restart on any panic, never carry on" (as I recommend) your policy?
11. **Extra test machines.** Memory-safety tools (MemorySanitizer, a build of Mesa and libwayland with sanitizers) need a machine and several hours; real GPU reset tests need the December hardware. Should any of this be scheduled before December, for instance a nightly fuzz job on a spare node?
12. **Scope of the "no reachable unwrap" goal (A1).** Classifying Smithay's roughly 1,400 `unwrap`/`expect` sites is a few days of reading. Is that wanted, or is "fuzz it well and restart on panic" enough?
13. **Memory ceiling and failed allocations (BC-14).** With a 6 GB address-space ceiling the compositor aborted on a failed allocation in all three IPC-fuzz seeds, and without a ceiling it did not. A Rust program aborts when an allocation fails, so any ceiling (a cgroup `memory.max` kills instead) turns "too much memory" into a restart. Do you want the compositor to survive memory pressure (large, careful change, and it cannot be fully done: Mesa and Smithay also allocate), or is "set a ceiling, restart on abort, restore the windows" the policy? This also decides whether D4's 256 MB per-stand-in cap should be lower.
14. **Window order after a restore.** In one of the 2-hour soak chunks, 2 of 7 exact-restore checks showed the same positions and sizes but a different window order (which window is on top of which). Is the stacking order part of "restore exact" for the hub, or only places and sizes? If it is, this needs a proper test (the saved file keeps the order, SOURCE `session.rs:78`).
